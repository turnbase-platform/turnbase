## 8. Findings

- **Scale-in is prompt and complete.** The load ended at about +845 s of wall clock (900 s of VU time at the 6.1% slew); the HPA's desired count fell to 2 at +910 s and the third Platform pod was gone (2/2) at +965 s, about 2 min after the end, against a bound of 10 min. The Game Host never left its minimum of 2: its peak was 75 sessions on two hosts, below the KEDA target.
- **Resources return below the pre-run level:** threads 81 to 71 (Platform pod) and 56 to 53 (Game Host); Redis keys 40 to 12 (the earlier 40 included the smoke runs' leftovers, which expired); rooms, sessions and sockets 0. `used_memory` is 9% higher (1.75 to 1.90 MB), within the 10%.
- **The Platform scale-out came at +117 s, 20 s to Ready,** at 40% CPU target during the ramp; no flapping.
- **Outliers:** the maximum `tb_game_start` is 43.6 s and the maximum `tb_move_rtt` 6.3 s, one sample each against p99 values of 157 ms and 114 ms. Not tied to a scale event in the timeline (the one scale-out was at +117 s); no cause established.
- **Slew:** the wall clock ran 6.1% slow.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
