# Soak scenario report, 2026-10-18

**Verdict:** 49 pairs (70% of the ramp-to-break ceiling of 70) held for 30 minutes with every latency target met and no restart, but the system did not stay flat: Redis memory grew 3.18 to 13.75 MB (4.3x) at about 0.36 MB per minute, platform threads 125 to 151, Game Host heap after GC 251 to 371 MB, HTTP p95 10 to 48 ms with requests per second down 25%, and after a burst of lost room ownership at 18 min the room and session gauges doubled and never came back down. Single-node, laptop result.

## 5. Result

Ceiling (pairs): 70 (load used: 49 = 70 x 7 / 10)
First to break: none
Evidence: no threshold crossed and no restart or OOM kill in 33 minutes at 49 pairs; the platform pod ran at 2.3 to 3.0 of 4 cores, the Game Host at 0.8 to 1.3 of 4. The trend is not flat though: Redis memory, platform threads, Game Host heap, HTTP latency and the room and session gauges all moved across the hold (§6, §8). Those are leak suspects, not breaks.

`Ceiling (pairs)` is one integer: the last step all of whose thresholds passed and that is not
generator-bound, or `<n> (not reached)` when no step failed. `First to break` is exactly one of:
`Game Host CPU`, `Espresso contention`, `DB pool`, `Redis`, `platform threads`, `ingress / frontend nginx`,
`k6 itself`, `memory (OOM)`, `none`. A report for `start-rate` writes `Ceiling (games/s): <n>` instead.

## 8. Findings

- **Redis memory grows steadily while the load is flat, with no plateau in 33 minutes.** 3.18 MB at 194 s to 13.75 MB at 1,910 s (+332%, 0.36 MB/min, linear), `connected_clients` flat at 5, 86,271 keys several minutes after the end. Suspected cause: per-room or per-session keys written on every game and never deleted. Not checked: which key family (`SCAN` by prefix), whether TTLs are set, whether it stops growing at the reconciliation interval.
- **The platform lost room ownership for about 35 rooms at once and the gauges never recovered.** At 21:22:38Z (17.5 min after the scenario start) the platform log shows 1 `ERROR` ("Game end of sessionId ... could not be detached", `IllegalStateException: room ... is not owned by this instance`, `RoomOwnershipService.nextVersion`) and 35 WARN "detach of room ... deferred: This instance does not own room" within 2 s. Within about a minute (the next gauge reading, 18.3 min) `rooms_active` rose 48 -> 97 and `sessions_active` 40 -> 75, and stayed there for the rest of the hold. Two `ReconciliationJob` "deleted orphaned session key family" lines followed at 21:34:29Z. several minutes after the end 53 rooms and 38 sessions were still reported with no socket open. Suspected cause: a stall (see `load-testing.md` §13 item 10) made the ownership lease lapse and the deferred detaches were not completed. Not checked: the lease TTL and renew interval, whether the stall coincides with the 300 s recurrence, whether the gauges or the rooms themselves are stale.
- **Latency rises and throughput falls across the hold at constant load.** HTTP p95 10 -> 48 ms and p99 18 -> 89 ms (monotonic, it rises before and after the 18 min step), requests per second 150 -> 113 (-25%), move p95 38 -> 52 ms, with platform CPU falling 2.96 -> 2.29 cores. Hikari active 2.95 -> 5.5 (pending max 4). Suspected cause: growth of a data structure the platform walks on each operation (the room and session sets, the Redis keyspace). Not checked: a profile at the end of the hold, Postgres statistics, the same load with a platform restart half way.
- **Game Host heap after GC grows 251 -> 371 MB** (+48%) with 53 to 56 threads throughout; the max heap rises 403 -> 529 MB. Suspected cause: retained per-session state (more sessions are reported active after 18 min, see above) or Espresso context growth. Not checked: a heap histogram, whether it stabilises with a longer hold.
- **Platform threads grow 58 -> 151** in steps (about 89 at 2.5 min, 124 at 7.5 min, 135 at 17.5 min, 149 at 20 min) and stay flat afterwards; `load-testing.md` §13 item 5 saw the same growth in `ramp-to-break`. Not a monotonic leak in this run; what the threads are is unchecked.
- **`ready_timeout` 68** in 214,488 games (0.03%), the same error as `load-testing.md` §13 item 4. Not clustered in this run: not checked by time.
- **Harness note.** `snapshot.sh` reads Redis only once, at the end. The start-of-hold Redis figures above come from a 30 s sampler (`redis-cli INFO memory` and `clients` through `kubectl exec`) run beside the soak, outside `loadtest/`; its file is not part of the run directory.
- `k6_tb_move_rtt_count` in Prometheus is a per-push figure (units not confirmed), so moves per second is taken from `summary.json`: 1,634,734 moves over 1,980 s = 826 per second.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
