## 8. Findings

- **A smooth daily shape is absorbed with no scaling activity.** From the Platform's one scale-out at +44 s (Ready +63 s; the setup's login burst, before the first rise) to the end, no replica count changed on either workload: 0 scale events in 37 minutes after the first minute, 0 flapping. The Game Host never exceeded 69 sessions on 2 hosts, under the KEDA target of 0.7 per host.
- **The Platform did not scale in at the troughs.** Its HPA held 3 replicas through both 5% troughs. The cause was not isolated: the troughs are brief (the day is a rise, a plateau and a fall with no hold at 5%), and a likely reason is that the load stayed above the HPA's scale-down threshold or inside its stabilisation window, which the run does not separate. The shape therefore showed no scale-in; `up-down` did (`2026-10-31-m18-up-down.md`). The consequence for the requirement the scenario was meant to test, "no scale-in mid-game failures", is that it was not exercised here.
- **Latency is flat across both days:** move p95 62 ms over the run and 68 ms on the plateau, game start p95 79 ms; no step approaches a threshold at 60% of the ceiling.
- **Slew:** the wall clock ran 6.5% slow.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
