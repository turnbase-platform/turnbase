Evidence: no threshold crossed in 30 minutes at 56 pairs (exit code 0). The drift criteria: HTTP p99 mean over the first 5 minutes of the hold (after the 120 s ramp) 36.3 ms, over the last 5 minutes 33.6 ms: **-7.2%**; p95 -10%; requests per second +11.6% (237 -> 265). `NFR-DBQ-002` (< 25%) holds. Secondary signals: `hikari_pending` mean 6.2 -> 8.2 (max 49) and Postgres 2.2 cores (1.69 to 2.21 per 5-minute segment): the pool queues and Postgres is at its limit, but not to the point of a failed target; the `PostgreSQL` rule needs a failing step to name a first to break.


`Ceiling (pairs)` is one integer: the last step all of whose thresholds passed and that is not
generator-bound, or `<n> (not reached)` when no step failed. `First to break` is exactly one of:
`Game Host CPU`, `Espresso contention`, `DB pool`, `Redis`, `platform threads`, `ingress / frontend nginx`,
`k6 itself`, `memory (OOM)`, `none`. A report for `start-rate` writes `Ceiling (games/s): <n>` instead.

## 8. Findings

1. **The Phase A and M16 drift is gone.** HTTP p99 -7.2% over the hold (M16 +122%), throughput +11.6% (M16 -26.5%): `IMP-M17-01` and `02` removed the scans of `room_participant` that grew with the table. `load-testing.md` §13 finding 15 is resolved.
2. **Postgres is still saturated at this load** (2.2 of 2 cores, pool queue 7 to 12 on average) but stable: the queue does not grow across the hold (`hikari_pending` 12.0 -> 8.3 over segments 2 to 6). 56 pairs is 33% of the new ramp ceiling, yet the Postgres CPU is the same as at 190 pairs: the soak load of 199 games/s is above M16's, because the games are not slowed by a full-table scan any more.
3. **Not rerun with the tuned chart.** `FR-DBQ-007` reruns the failing scenarios only; this one did not name `PostgreSQL` or `DB pool`.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
