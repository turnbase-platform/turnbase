# Up-down scenario report, M16 re-verification, 2026-10-21

**Verdict:** After the load fell to zero, WebSockets, rooms, sessions and DB connections were at 0 within 30 s, platform threads fell from 152 to 67 (JVM live threads) within 60 s and stood at 82 against 76 before (+7.9%, `/proc` census), and the 36,047 stranded Redis keys of Phase A are gone: `DBSIZE` was 5 before, 6 at five minutes after the load (one extra key with a TTL, 5 again when read a few minutes later), so the Redis criterion holds for every room key and misses the letter of "within 1%" by that one key. 0 errors, no restart, no ownership error. Single-node, laptop result.

## 5. Result

Ceiling (pairs): 80 (load used: 56 = 80 x 7 / 10)
First to break: none
Evidence: no threshold crossed and no restart or OOM kill in 20 minutes at up to 56 pairs; the platform pod ran at 2.4 to 2.7 of 4 cores during the hold and the Game Host at 0.9 of 4. The Hikari pool reached 10 of 10 busy with up to 59 pending at the hold (`hikari_pending` max 59), as in `ramp-to-break`. What the scenario measures is the release, in §6 and §8.

`Ceiling (pairs)` is one integer: the last step all of whose thresholds passed and that is not
generator-bound, or `<n> (not reached)` when no step failed. `First to break` is exactly one of:
`Game Host CPU`, `Espresso contention`, `DB pool`, `Redis`, `platform threads`, `ingress / frontend nginx`,
`k6 itself`, `memory (OOM)`, `none`. A report for `start-rate` writes `Ceiling (games/s): <n>` instead.

## 8. Findings

Criteria (`IMP-M16-15`):

| Criterion | Result |
|---|---|
| No `not owned by this instance` ERROR in the platform log | **pass**: 0 occurrences (`platform.log` in the run directory) |
| Redis keyspace returns to its pre-run size within 5 minutes after the load (within 1%) | **fail by the letter, no leak**: 6 keys at 5 minutes against 5 before (+1 key with a TTL, not a room key), 5 when read a few minutes later; every room key (about 460 at the maximum) was gone within 30 s of the end of the load; `used_memory` 2.8% below the before value |
| Platform threads idle-after within 10% of idle-before | **pass**: 82 against 76 (+7.9%, `/proc`); the Prometheus JVM gauge reads 67 against 61 (+9.8%) |
| No Game Host restart | **pass**: 0 restarts, no wedge line |
| Heap and HTTP p99 drift over the soak | measured by `soak` (`2026-10-21-m16-soak.md`) |

- **The Phase A leaks of this scenario are closed.** Sockets, rooms, sessions and DB connections at 0 within 30 s (Phase A: the same); platform threads 152 -> 67 within 60 s and 82 in the census (Phase A: 109 against 57 before, stuck); Redis keys 5 (Phase A: 36,047, two per room, never removed). `load-testing.md` §13 items 13 and 17.
- **One extra Redis key after the load.** `keys=6,expires=4` at five minutes against `keys=5,expires=3`, gone when read a few minutes later; the soak's `census-after` read `keys=6,expires=4` too. By `avg_ttl` (43.2 million ms over four keys) the extra key had a TTL of the same order as the 24 h `host:<id>` key; which key it is was not checked. It is not a room key and does not grow with the load; whether the criterion should count it is the owner's reading of "within 1%". Phase B note: identify it.
- **The Game Host's thread count after the load** is 57 (census) against 52 before, 43 to 44 by the Prometheus gauge: no growth.
- **The cold boot costs 3.3 cores.** The Game Host reached 3.34 cores in the first segment (the first guest boots, not isolated), then 0.9 at the hold. Not a finding about release; recorded for the contexts item.
- **Harness note.** The `before` census was taken 60 s after provisioning on a stack reset beforehand, so the Game Host had not yet booted a guest (52 threads): its `after / before` ratio compares a warm JVM with a cold one and is not a criterion.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
