# `soak`, M18 single-instance re-verification, `IMP-M18-07`, 2026-10-24

**Verdict:** 56 pairs held for 30 minutes on the M18 build with every latency target met, 0 errors and no restart; **HTTP p99 drifted +22% (17.7 -> 21.5 ms)** over the hold, under the 25% of `NFR-DBQ-002`, while requests per second rose 27%; from the second 5-minute segment on it is flat (20 -> 21 ms, +5%). Nothing crossed a threshold, the pool queue stayed at 0.3 to 3.8 on average (max 16) and Postgres at 1.9 of 4 cores, so the first to break is `none`. Single-node, laptop result.

Evidence: no threshold crossed in 30 minutes at 56 pairs (exit code 0). The drift criteria: HTTP p99 mean over the first 5 minutes of the hold (after the 120 s ramp) 17.7 ms, over the last 5 minutes 21.5 ms: **+21.8%**; p95 11.8 -> 14.2 ms (+20%); requests per second 228 -> 290 (+27%). `NFR-DBQ-002` (< 25%) holds. The first window overlaps the warm-up after the reset (Game Host 1.8 cores for the first 10 minutes, then 1.3 to 1.4; HTTP load 169 -> 295 requests/s over the first 15 minutes), so the rise is the warm-up and not growth over the hold: segments 3 to 7 (minutes 10 to 33) give 20, 21, 21, 21 and 21 ms. Secondary signals: `hikari_pending` mean 1.95 -> 1.75 (max 16), Postgres 1.7 to 1.9 of 4 cores in every segment from the second on, platform threads flat at 164 to 172.

`Ceiling (pairs)` is one integer: the last step all of whose thresholds passed and that is not
generator-bound, or `<n> (not reached)` when no step failed. `First to break` is exactly one of:
`Game Host CPU`, `Espresso contention`, `DB pool`, `Redis`, `platform threads`, `ingress / frontend nginx`,
`k6 itself`, `memory (OOM)`, `none`. A report for `start-rate` writes `Ceiling (games/s): <n>` instead.

## 8. Findings

1. **The drift stays under the bar and is the warm-up.** +21.8% between the first and last 5 minutes of the hold (M17 -7.2%, M16 +122%), but the first window is the warm-up after a reset and the last five segments are flat. `NFR-DBQ-002` holds; `load-testing.md` §13 finding 15 stays resolved.
2. **The same load costs less database.** The 56-pair load plays 217 games/s (M17 182 over the same time base) with Postgres at 1.9 of 4 cores and the pool queue 0.3 to 3.8 (M17: 2.2 of 2 cores, queue 7 to 12): `NFR-DBQ-001` for this scenario holds with `none`.
3. **Not generator-bound, no restart.**

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
