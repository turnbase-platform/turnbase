Ceiling (pairs): 80 (load used: 56 = 80 x 7 / 10)

Evidence: no threshold crossed, no restart and no OOM kill in 32 minutes at 56 pairs; the platform pod ran at 1.9 to 2.5 of 4 cores, the Game Host at 0.6 to 0.9 of 4, Postgres at its 2-core limit. generator-bound, or `<n> (not reached)` when no step failed. `First to break` is exactly one of:
`Game Host CPU`, `Espresso contention`, `DB pool`, `Redis`, `platform threads`, `ingress / frontend nginx`,

- **Latency still rises and throughput falls across the hold at constant load, and the cause is visible in Postgres.** HTTP p99 77 -> 172 ms, requests per second 160.6 -> 118.0, platform CPU 2.44 -> 1.94 cores: the platform waits instead of working. `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE)` of the occupancy subquery at the end of the run: `Seq Scan on room_participant`, 85,270 rows removed by the filter, 7.0 ms. The subquery has no `room_id` restriction (it counts `role = 'PLAYER' and left_at is null` for every team in the table, then joins), and the lookup by `user_id` has no index (the primary key is `(room_id, user_id)`; plan `Seq Scan`), so each call costs time linear in the table, which holds every closed room's participants (85,270 rows at the end; 119,330 in attempt 1, where it cost 9.4 ms). Three such statements account for 2,367 s of execution time over a 1,926 s run on a 2-core Postgres, and the pool is 10 of 10 busy with about 50 waiting from the first 5 minutes. This is the remainder of `load-testing.md` §13 item 15: the Redis and reconciliation walks `IMP-M16-08` and `09` removed are gone (Redis flat), the Postgres ones are not. Not changed here (measurement only). Phase B note.
- **Phase A leaks that are gone:** Redis memory flat (item 13), Game Host heap flat (item 16), gauges not doubled and no ownership loss (item 14), platform threads flat after 5 minutes (item 5), `ready_timeout` 0 (item 4).
- **Longest start.** The longest `game_start` was 7,243 ms, not checked by time (most likely a cold start after the reset); no wedge.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
