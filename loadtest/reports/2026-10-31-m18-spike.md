# `spike`, Phase B, `IMP-M18-15`, 2026-10-31

**Verdict:** A step from 17 to 170 pairs (10% to 100% of the 170-pair scaled `ramp-to-break` ceiling) within 30 s, held for 10 min, passes every `load-testing.md` §6 threshold in the spike step: move p95 112 ms, create 95 ms, join 103 ms, game start 142 ms, error rate 0.0017%. The Game Host's third replica was Ready 142 s after the rise began (NFR-ASC-001 met), and `CORRECTNESS: PASS`. The Game Host then scaled in again during the hold, 156 s after it was Ready, with the load unchanged (flapping). Single-node, laptop result.

## 8. Findings

- **The spike is absorbed by thresholds, and the scale-out reacts inside the bound.** 17 to 170 pairs in 30 s: no threshold crossed in any step. The Game Host's third replica was Running at +410 s and Ready at +425 s from the scenario start, the rise having begun at about +283 s (300 s of VU time at the 5.7% slew): 142 s from the start of the rise, 16 s of it the pod's own boot (Spring `Started` in about 10 s).
- **Platform did not scale on the spike.** The HPA went to three replicas 12 s after the scenario start, on the setup's login burst of 340 users, before the base step, and stayed there: the spike met three replicas, so the Platform's reaction to the spike itself is not measured by this run (the first replica to be Ready was at +27 s, 7 minutes before the spike). The base step ran at 0.87 busiest-pod cores on three replicas.
- **Game Host flapped during the hold.** Scale-in began at +581 s (pod `…m22fq` Terminating), 156 s after it was Ready and about 270 s into the hold, with the load at 170 pairs. The KEDA trigger is a point maximum of the sessions utilisation (`ramp-to-break` finding on the spikiness of the series); nothing else changed in the load. Correctness held: no game lost, no gap, no duplicate (below). The drain and the reassignment of the third host's sessions are therefore exercised under load, and cost nothing visible in the thresholds.
- **Some cold-start cost, if any, is not separable.** The Game Host's new third replica took its first sessions during the spike's hold. The step's `tb_game_start` p99 of 256 ms against 83 ms in the base step is the only trace; the `ramp-to-break` run's 2.1 s start tail did not repeat. Not enough to call it a result either way.
- **Thresholds recovered with no delay:** the first `tail` sample is in the window; the `drop` step has no sub-metrics, and `tail` reads move p95 8 ms and game start 20 ms, better than `base`.
- **Slew:** the wall clock ran 5.7% slow; wall offsets of the timeline are that much short of the VU time.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile. The `spike` scenario has no aborting threshold.
