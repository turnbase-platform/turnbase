# Phase B baseline (`parallelism` and `ramp-to-break`), `IMP-M18-02`, 2026-10-24

**Verdict:** With PostgreSQL at 4 CPU / 2 GiB and the Game Host at 2 CPU, on the unchanged `e52c1f4` platform build, `parallelism` passes every valid step up to 128 pairs (M17: failed at 128, ceiling 64) and `ramp-to-break` reaches **220 pairs** (M17: 170, 190 tuned), aborting at 230 on a `tb_room_join` p95 of 218 ms. PostgreSQL now runs at 2.1 to 2.4 of 4 cores, below the 95% (3.8) of the amended rule, while 80 to 180 requests wait for a connection, so the first to break is **DB pool**; the platform is at 3.7 to 3.8 of 4 cores and is the next limit. Single-node, laptop result.

## 5. Result

`parallelism`
Ceiling (pairs): 128
First to break: none
Evidence: every valid step passes, including 128 pairs, which failed in M17 (`tb_room_join` 232 ms, `tb_room_create` 210 ms). The 256-pair step is cut short and not valid, so the ceiling is the last valid passing step. No restart, no 5xx, no generator-bound segment. The limits now in sight: the 10-connection pool queues (`hikari_pending` max 148 at 128 pairs) with Postgres at 2.13 of 4 cores (53%), and the platform is at 3.54 of 4. Peak 1,690 moves/s at 128 pairs (1,683 at 64), 5% below M17 B's 1,733 and 1,711 at the same steps: the Game Host now has half the cores, and the platform CPU, not Postgres, is what the extra database cores meet.

`ramp-to-break`
Ceiling (pairs): 220
First to break: DB pool
Evidence: the only metric that crossed is `tb_room_join` (p95 217.6 ms at 230 pairs, 171 ms at 220), while moves, creates, lobby events and game starts stay inside their targets. At the failing step connections wait (`hikari_pending` max 170, mean about 100) with Postgres at 2.3 to 2.4 of 4 cores (about 59%), below the amended 95% (3.8) threshold of `load-testing.md` §8, so the rule names `DB pool`, not `PostgreSQL` (M17: `PostgreSQL` at 2.0 to 2.2 of 2). The platform is at 3.76 of 4 cores (94%), the next limit. No generator-bound segment, no 5xx.

Capacity statements:

- 500 concurrent sessions: **not reached.** Ceiling 220 pairs = 220 sessions at most (`sessions_active` max 170).
- 5,000 WebSockets: **not reached; ceiling in sockets: 440** (2 x 220; 470 open at the abort). The ceiling is below 1,000 pairs, so the idle-socket run was not made (protocol, `IMP-M15-11` step 3).

`Ceiling (pairs)` is one integer: the last step all of whose thresholds passed and that is not
generator-bound, or `<n> (not reached)` when no step failed. `First to break` is exactly one of:
`Game Host CPU`, `Espresso contention`, `DB pool`, `Redis`, `platform threads`, `ingress / frontend nginx`,
`k6 itself`, `memory (OOM)`, `none`. A report for `start-rate` writes `Ceiling (games/s): <n>` instead.

## 8. Findings

1. **The extra Postgres cores moved the ceiling.** `parallelism` passes 128 pairs (M17 failed there) and `ramp-to-break` reaches 220 pairs (M17: 170, 190 with the 2 CPU settings), with the join p95 at 170 pairs 134 ms against 178 ms and Postgres using 2.1 to 2.4 of its 4 cores against the whole of its 2. This confirms `load-testing-phase-b.md` §3.1: Postgres CPU was the M17 limit. The Game Host at 2 CPU did not matter: it uses 1.2 of 2 cores.
2. **The next limits are the connection pool and the platform's CPU.** With Postgres at about 55% the pool of 10 still queues (`hikari_pending` max 148 to 183) and the platform is at 3.5 to 3.8 of 4 cores. Peak moves/s did not rise (1,690 to 1,917 per step against 1,733 to 1,779 in M17): the platform CPU per move is unchanged, and the work of `IMP-M18-03` to `05` (fewer statements, a participant cache, one Redis round trip) is what lowers it. The pool size stays 10 until the Track 2 runs (`load-testing-phase-b.md` §4.5).
3. **The 256-pair step of `parallelism` is cut short again** (finding 4 of `2026-10-24-m17-parallelism.md`); the cause is not investigated here, a baseline records and does not fix. The ceiling line uses steps 1 to 128.
4. **`NFR-DBQ-001` read with the amended rule:** `parallelism` names `none`, `ramp-to-break` names `DB pool`; neither names `PostgreSQL`. This is a measurement, not a verdict on the Phase B goals.
5. **Capacity targets** (500 sessions, 5,000 WebSockets) remain unreached on one node: 170 sessions and 470 sockets at the abort.

## 9. Constants

- This is a single-node, laptop ceiling: Docker Desktop Kubernetes on one WSL2 VM shared with the load generator. It is the ceiling of this machine, not of the platform.
- Latency is measured per request from a closed-loop client at the configured think time, so coordinated omission applies: when the system slows, the client issues fewer requests and the percentiles understate the delay a constant-rate client would see. `start-rate` is open-loop.
- One step is generator-bound when the k6 process averages more than 80% of its pinned cores after the settle window; such a step never counts as the platform's breaking point.
- The first `SETTLE_SECONDS` of every step are excluded from that step's thresholds.
- A crossed threshold aborts the run no earlier than 30 s after the test start (`delayAbortEval` counts from the test start, not from the step), so a step's first samples after its settle window can trip a one-sample percentile.
